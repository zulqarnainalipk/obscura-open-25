\Title{OBSCURA-Open-25: An Open Multi-Target Satellite Dataset and Facility-Grouped Validation Guidelines for Industrial Emission Sites}

\Author{Zulqarnain Ali $^{1}$}
\AuthorNames{Zulqarnain Ali}
\address{%
$^{1}$ \quad Affiliation to be added; ORCID 0009-0009-0281-882X}
\corres{Correspondence: zulqar445ali@gmail.com}

\abstract{Few open datasets pair facility-scale satellite imagery with several co-measured gases, and models built on such data are usually validated with random splits. We release OBSCURA-Open-25: 1355 samples from 25 industrial facilities in 15 countries. Each sample pairs a 14-layer Sentinel-2 image chip with Sentinel-5P methane, nitrogen dioxide and carbon monoxide enhancements, an aerosol index and the maximum detected fire brightness temperature, plus weather, terrain and spectral features and a record of all 1825 attempted windows. A Python package gives access and a grouped-validation protocol. In a case study on the 25 selected facilities, random-split evaluation understates the error relative to facility-grouped evaluation by 11\% to 38\%. For methane and fire, a model using only elevation and slope reproduces the full model's random-split RMSE, consistent with facility recognition. Under a grouped split only the aerosol index is clearly better than the baselines. Re-running the collection reproduced 15 of 15 sampled windows. The targets are satellite products, not ground truth.}

\keyword{Earth observation; Sentinel-2; Sentinel-5P; methane; industrial emissions; cross-validation; spatial leakage; open data}

\section{Summary}

Sentinel-5P measures methane, nitrogen dioxide, carbon monoxide and aerosols almost daily worldwide \cite{veefkind}, but cloud and quality screening often remove the reading. Open benchmarks pair Sentinel-2 imagery with labelled methane sources \cite{zhu} or plumes \cite{vaughan,allen}, or with Sentinel-5P nitrogen dioxide \cite{scheibenreif}. To our knowledge none offers facility-level chips with several co-measured gases and a record of every attempted observation.

The note offers the dataset with its window record, a Python package, and a validation protocol showing why whole facilities must be held out \cite{roberts}. All numbers come from the Zenodo record and the code at \url{https://github.com/zulqarnainalipk/obscura-open-25}.

\section{Data Description}

\textbf{Samples.} A facility has a fixed centre and a square image box (1.5 to 7.75 km), sized per facility and checked by hand against imagery. Every second 5-day window from 1 January 2022 to 27 December 2023 was tried (73 per facility, 1825 in all). In each window the Sentinel-2 date with the least cloud over the box is chosen. Sentinel-5P granules and FIRMS detections from one day before to one day after that date are paired with it. A window becomes a sample if the box has at most 30\% cloud and at least one gas target was measured: 1355 samples (74\%). The other 470 windows had too much cloud (441), no scene (11), or failed other checks (18). The 25 facilities (Figure~\ref{fig1}a) were selected with a quality score for samples, gas availability, cloud, seasons and box size (\texttt{docs/SELECTION.md}).

\textbf{Chips.} Each chip is a $384\times384$ GeoTIFF with 14 layers in this order: Sentinel-2 L2A bands B1 to B8, B8A, B9, B11, B12 (reflectance times 10,000) \cite{drusch}, the scene classification and the cloud probability. They are unsigned 16-bit with nodata 0, no cloud mask, a local UTM grid and pixels of 3.9 to 20.2 m. The grid origin is shifted by a random amount below 60 m in x and y, (chip centre to facility centre: median 48 m, largest 84 m).

\textbf{Targets.} Each gas enhancement is the mean of land pixels in a 10 km window minus the median of land pixels in a 30 to 75 km ring that leaves out areas near other facilities (land from MOD44W \cite{carroll}). Inputs are the Earth Engine Level-3 offline Sentinel-5P collections (1.1 km) \cite{veefkind,gorelick}: bias-corrected methane mixing ratio (ppb), tropospheric nitrogen dioxide column (\textmu mol\,m$^{-2}$) and carbon monoxide column (mmol\,m$^{-2}$). The quality filter is the collection's own; we added none. Granules of the paired days are averaged per pixel. The aerosol index (unitless, UV absorbing) is the window mean. Fire is the highest detected MODIS brightness temperature (T21, K) \cite{giglio} in the near-real-time FIRMS collection inside the 10 km window on the paired days (all confidence levels), and 0 when none was reported. Zero means no detection, not that no fire occurred (85\% of samples), so the value mixes detection and brightness. In an exploratory grouped analysis a classifier discriminated detection no better than chance (AUC 0.41, interval 0.27 to 0.53). Methane is present in 49\% of samples and the other gases in 99\%.

\textbf{Features.} The 29 features are month (sine, cosine), ERA5-Land weather \cite{munoz}, elevation and slope \cite{dem}, chip cloud share and 18 spectral indices. 

\section{Data Records and Usage Guidelines}

The record has 40 files (2.2 GB) under CC BY 4.0: tables, 25 chip archives, documentation and SHA-256 checksums. Rules:

\begin{enumerate}[labelsep=2mm]
\item Read \texttt{sample\_id} as text; four identifiers look like numbers.
\item Fit and score each target only where it exists.
\item Validate by facility, never by row.
\end{enumerate}

Install with \texttt{pip install -e ".[analysis]"} (Python 3.9 or newer); product identifiers, formulas and checks are in \texttt{docs/}. Workflow:
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
from obscura_open25 import Dataset, compare_splits
ds = Dataset("data")
print(compare_splits(ds).round(3))
\end{verbatim}}

\section{Case Study: Random Versus Facility-Grouped Validation}

A scikit-learn gradient-boosting regressor (untuned, no preprocessing; settings in the repository) was fitted per target and fold on the 29 features. Grouped folds hold exactly five facilities, random folds random rows, and each split is repeated for five seeds (partitions). The error is the RMSE of the pooled out-of-fold predictions on the same measured rows, in z-score units, averaged over seeds; ``understated by'' is computed from unrounded values (the table shows rounded ones). The baselines are zero (a fixed reference: the constants in \texttt{scaling.json} were computed once, outside the folds, so they are not independent of the test folds) and the mean of the training folds, the primary baseline.

Every target looks better under a random split. Under a grouped split only the aerosol index beats the training-fold mean with an interval that excludes zero (grouped minus fold-mean RMSE $-0.49$ to $-0.27$; intervals for all targets in \texttt{docs/EVALUATION.md}); for the other targets the interval includes zero, and nitrogen dioxide (0.69 against 0.71) is marginal. Fire reverses: the random split beats zero (0.64 against 0.91), the grouped split does not (1.04). The spread over seeds is at most 0.034 and reflects fold assignment only.

A model saw only elevation and slope, constant within a facility but taking only 17 distinct combinations over 25 facilities. Under a random split it matches the full model for methane (0.89 against 0.89) and fire (0.65 against 0.64); under a grouped split it is no better than the baselines (1.08 for both). A predictor using only the mean of the facility's own training rows (a diagnostic: it needs the facility in training) does as well or better for methane, carbon monoxide and fire (0.84, 0.65, 0.63). This is consistent with facility recognition but does not prove it.

\section{Technical Validation}

All files match their SHA-256 checksums, and the 1355 samples and 1355 chips correspond one to one. Fourteen consistency checks passed. Fifteen samples at 15 facilities were rebuilt from the public archives; every numeric field, scene and granule identifier and chip pixel was identical. This repeats the same procedure; it is not independent and was not run for all samples.

\section{Limitations and Intended Use}

The targets are satellite proxies, not emission rates or ground concentrations. Enhancements near zero are mostly retrieval noise (45\% of methane values are negative). The 25 facilities were selected for quality; grouped evaluation tests transfer within this curated set, and cloudier or sparser sites may behave differently. The results concern one model and 25 facilities; they show a difference between validation schemes, not a general cause. Suited to generalisation research, not emission quantification. The main lesson: hold out whole facilities.
